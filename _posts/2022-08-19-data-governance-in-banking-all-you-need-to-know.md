<h3><strong>Data Governance in Banking — All You Need to Know</strong></h3><p>This article is for those who are interested in Data Governance in Banking or Data Governance Roles in banking.</p><h3><strong>What is Data Governance?</strong></h3><p>More and more financial institutions are finding themselves overwhelmed with mounting quantities of datasets and are simply unable to manage this critical asset at such a large scale. Because of this, some organizations are beginning to recognize the importance of incorporating Data Governance programs into their operations. Data Governance sets the tone for the overall corporate mindset and culture, by establishing enterprise-wide data norms pertaining to documentation, data capturing, ownership, and access privileges.</p><figure><img alt="Data Governance is at the center of a lot of other disciplines that focus on getting in control of your data. — DAMA" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/866/0*t3Og-hNuJKc_6Ln0.png" /><figcaption>Data Governance is at the center of a lot of other disciplines that focus on getting in control of your data.</figcaption></figure><h3><strong>Why Data Governance Matters</strong></h3><p>Why do banks implement data governance? To be frank, the first and most important reason is for compliance and regulation.</p><p>Data governance provides a platform for meeting the demands of government regulations. Relevant legislation and regulations requiring consistent compliance, include the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Comprehensive Capital Analysis and Review (CCAR) in the United States, Bank Secrecy Act/Anti-Money Laundering legislation (BSA/AML), and Current Expected Credit Loss model (CECL). When determining the scope of the data governance project, banks usually consider compliance obligations and build risk-related data-control capabilities, which, for example, enable automated regulatory reporting required by the Federal Reserve.</p><p>The primary purpose of data governance does not sound exciting but, on the side, an effective data governance strategy provides many other benefits to an organization, including</p><ol><li>A common understanding of data: Data governance provides detailed policy and procedures for data. The data structure, terminology, and relationship will be documented and accessible to users</li><li>Accessibility of data: Data governance gives the user a way to quickly find the right data. Banks have thousands of tables of data and it is extremely difficult to find the right table and the right information without good data governance</li><li>Improved data quality: Data governance monitors data accuracy, completeness, consistency, timeliness, validity, and uniqueness. Good quality data can power accurate analysis, allowing data stewards to quickly identify data issues and effectively monitor security breaches.</li></ol><h3><strong>Data Governance Roles</strong></h3><p>The individuals who are responsible for data need to have clearly defined roles and responsibilities in an organization. On the organizational level, a bank usually has a dedicated data governance team and data governance professionals in every department.</p><p><strong>Data Officers:</strong></p><p>Data management teams are comprised of data officers who are responsible for the administration of data governance. In practice, the whole data governance project should be guided and managed by data officers. It is the responsibility of the data officer to reinforce the tone set at the top by executive management. This means they:</p><ol><li>Define data strategy, policies, and procedures</li><li>Ensure ownership across different data domains in the data life cycle</li><li>Own the data management tools</li><li>Provide support for solving identified data issues.</li></ol><p>At the department level, each department should have at least the following two roles.</p><p><strong>Data Owners:</strong></p><p>A data owner has overall accountability for the data assets within their functional area. They make sure that the data within the specific domain is well managed. The data owner is usually a senior business manager in the company, for example, the head of the credit approval department could be the owner of credit data. Data owners are not involved on a daily basis in data governance activities, however, they are supported in the execution of their role by data stewards.</p><p><strong>Data Stewards</strong></p><p>A data steward is responsible for the day-to-day management of data in a specific functional domain or department, including maintaining data integrity and data quality. Data stewards usually maintain a strong knowledge of relevant policies and procedures, as well as the data use in their domain. Thus, data stewards usually define the functional data requirements and serve as points of contact for related projects.</p><p>In addition, the IT department will support data governance projects as data custodians.</p><p><strong>Data Custodians</strong></p><p>A data custodian is a type of job role related to the aggregation, storage, and use of data sets. It is very much an IT role and staff in this role are responsible for the actual technology implementation to maintain data governance, rather than the identification of the why and how questions.</p><h3><strong>Key Skills Required</strong></h3><p>When a bank is recruiting for their data governance office, they will usually refer to data officers as data owners, data stewards and data custodians. These are not full-time jobs, they are all functions that employees carry within an organization. The traits/skills that banks usually look for are:</p><ol><li>A basic understanding of the data capabilities, such as data quality, data lineage, data modeling, and data profiling</li><li>Experience with data architecture and how data is organized within the organization, such as a data warehouse and data lake</li><li>An ability to work cross-functionally; the data management office exists in the back office and serves all internal teams. Therefore, being able to learn quickly and adapt to changes are important aspects of this role</li><li>An appreciation of big data technologies</li><li>An ability to manipulate data, for example, use/understand SQL</li><li>An understanding of product development and testing. Usually, data management tools are developed either through internal product development or third-party software. As the owner of the tools, it is best to have a certain level of product management knowledge</li><li>Ability to capture and identify requirements that meet business needs.</li></ol><p>If you don’t have all the knowledge and skills that are required for the job, that’s perfectly fine.
